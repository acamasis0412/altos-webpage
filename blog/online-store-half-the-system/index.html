Your Online Store Is Only Half the System

AC
Al Camasis
Founder, Altos · August 31, 2026 · 4 min read

Launching a new online store feels like the finish line. In our experience, it is the easy half. The store is the part everyone sees: the products, the checkout, the polish. The half that actually decides whether the business runs smoothly is the part nobody sees, which is everything the store is connected to.

A storefront is a window, not a system

On its own, a store is a window onto your products. It shows what you sell and takes orders. But by itself it knows nothing about how much stock you actually have, what you are selling on other channels, or what happens after the "buy" button. It can look perfect and still be an island. And an island is where the problems start.

The problems all live in the disconnect

When a store is not connected to the rest of your operation, the same issues show up every time. You oversell, because the store keeps selling something the warehouse ran out of yesterday. Someone re-keys orders by hand into another system, which is slow and quietly error-prone. Stock drifts between channels because nothing keeps them in step. And nobody has a single, trustworthy view of what is actually in stock right now. None of these are store problems. They are connection problems.

One source of truth is the real goal

What you actually want is simple to say and harder to build: stock, orders, and pricing that stay in sync automatically across every place you sell. Sell one unit anywhere, and every channel knows immediately. The store reflects reality, and reality updates the store, with no spreadsheets and no midnight reconciling. That single source of truth is what makes a store feel effortless to run.

The platform is not the hard part

Standing up a store on Shopify, WooCommerce, or anything else is well-trodden ground. The value, and the difficulty, is wiring that store into your inventory, your orders, and your other channels so the whole thing behaves as one system. That is integration, and it is where most of the real engineering lives, and most of the payoff. It is also the part a cheap theme setup skips entirely, which is exactly why those stores end up as expensive islands.

Ask the connection questions before you launch

So before you launch a new store, the useful questions are not about how it looks. They are: where does stock actually live, and what updates it? What happens the moment an order comes in? How do all your channels stay in step? What breaks when one system is down, and how does it recover? Answer those, and the store becomes a real asset instead of one more thing to keep in sync by hand.

A storefront is only half the system. The connected half is what makes it actually work, and it is the half we build. If you are launching a store, or you already have one that feels like an island, that is the part we are here for.
